    Getting started, step 2 of 4

    First steps: the Burgers equation in ten minutes

    Simulate the viscous Burgers equation, plot its evolution and measure its kinetic energy. Then change one line at a time and see what changes. The longer path through one problem, from stability to a simulation of convection rolls, is Convection between two plates: from onset to simulation.

    On this page

    1. Install
    2. The equation
    3. Simulate and plot
    4. The kinetic energy
    5. Changes to try
    6. Next

    Time: ten minutes; the script runs in a few seconds. Script: t00_first_steps.py.

    1. Install

    The last line says what this machine can run; Installation has the other ways to install.

    2. The equation

    A wave u(x,t) on a periodic line of length 2π steepens under its own advection and is held back by viscosity ν:

    ∂tu+u ∂xu = ν ∂x²u, u(x,0) = sinx.

    You write the domain, the unknown, the parameter and the equation. m.explain() prints how Conatus will treat each term. The linear term is implicit, the product u*dx(u) is explicit, and the product is dealiased by the 3/2 rule:

    3. Simulate and plot

    Specify the resolution N, the time-stepping scheme and the time step dt. run(until=t) advances to time t, which must be a whole number of steps.

    4. The kinetic energy

    The kinetic energy E = 1/2∫u² dx starts at π/2 and decreases as viscosity dissipates the steepening front.

    The second line compares E with the exact solution, given by the Cole–Hopf transformation (Cole 1951; Hopf 1950). The code of that comparison is below.

    Verification against the exact solution

    The Cole–Hopf transformation turns the Burgers equation into the heat equation, whose solution from u(x,0) = sinx is a series of Bessel functions. The script computes the exact energy from that series and checks the simulation against it to 10⁻⁹ relative.

    5. Changes to try

    Change one line of the script, run it again and read what happens.

    1. Less viscosity. cn.Model(..., nu=0.05) makes the front twice as steep. Change the 0.1 of cole_hopf_energy too: the energy still matches the exact solution, to 2×10⁻¹⁰.
    2. Too few modes. With m.simulate(N=32, ...) the front is not resolved. Conatus keeps the resolution you gave and warns once, with an UnderResolvedWarning that names the unknown, the direction and the spectral tail. The energy is then right to about 4×10⁻⁶.
    3. A larger time step. dt=0.05 takes 40 steps instead of 2000. The scheme is third order in time, so the energy error grows to about 4×10⁻⁶. sim.cfl(safety=0.5) suggests a stable step; the run keeps the dt you gave.
    4. Another equation. Drop the nonlinear term: m.equations("dt(u) - nu*lap(u) = 0") is the heat equation, and the energy decays exactly as E(t) = π/2e−2νt.
