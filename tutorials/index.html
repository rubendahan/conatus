    Tutorials

    Tutorials

    Each tutorial is a plain Python script, explained piece by piece, that ends by comparing its result with a published value or a closed form; what you see on the page is what the script printed. New to Conatus? Follow the three tutorials of Start here in order.

    Start here

    1. First steps: the Burgers equation in ten minutes

      Ten minutes: one equation written as on paper, a simulation, a plot, one physical number, four guided changes.

      Checked against Cole (1951), Hopf (1950).

    2. A first eigenvalue problem: Orr–Sommerfeld

      A native model, conditions by wall name, eigenvalues with residuals, critical parameters, verify.

      Checked against Orszag (1971).

    3. Convection between two plates: from onset to simulation

      The whole path once: a native model, explain(), stability, the onset by critical, a simulation, the heat flux, the Methods paragraph, the run registry.

      Checked against Reid & Harris (1958).

    Simulation

    1. A first initial-value problem: Rayleigh–Bénard 2D, CPU then GPU

      A simulation on CPU, then on GPU; time steppers, CFL, the Methods paragraph.

      Checked against linear growth rate, CPU/GPU agreement.

    2. Polar geometry: the modes of a drum

      Polar geometry: the modes of a drum on cn.Disk; extensions: ready pipe and Taylor–Couette problems.

      Checked against Bessel zeros, Meseguer & Trefethen (2003), Recktenwald et al. (1993).

    3. Lagrangian tracers and a numerical dye

      Tracers with sim.particles, a dye declared by m.dye and recorded by sim.dye: spectral velocity at the tracers, observed order in time, a dye whose mass is measured.

      Checked against Taylor–Green and Couette closed forms.

    Stability and sensitivity

    1. Symmetries and symmetry subspaces

      Linearizing a native model, symmetry subspaces: the same eigenvalue with half the unknowns.

      Checked against Reid & Harris (1958).

    2. Where a global mode is born, and what the data say about it

      Direct and adjoint global modes, wavemaker, base-flow sensitivity; DMD in the exact norm.

      Checked against Chomaz (2005) closed forms, Towne et al. (2018).

    Nonlinear dynamics

    1. Nonlinear boundary-value problems and continuation: Bratu

      A nonlinear boundary-value model, Newton on the exact Jacobian, pseudo-arclength continuation, folds.

      Checked against Bratu (1914), Gelfand (1963).

    2. Invariant solutions: Kuramoto–Sivashinsky, L = 22

      Equilibria, travelling waves and periodic orbits of a native model by Newton–Krylov–hookstep, Floquet exponents.

      Checked against Cvitanović et al. (2010), Ding & Cvitanović (2016).

    3. Bifurcations: the Lorenz equilibria

      The Lorenz equilibria through a pitchfork and a Hopf point; extensions: cycles, codimension 2, Landau and Ginzburg–Landau.

      Checked against Lorenz (1963), Kuznetsov (2004), Cross & Hohenberg (1993).

    4. Chaos and ensembles: the Lyapunov spectrum of Lorenz

      The Lyapunov spectrum and Kaplan–Yorke dimension of Lorenz; extensions: covariant vectors, Poincaré map, basins.

      Checked against Sprott (Lorenz), exact divergence.

    5. Covariant Lyapunov vectors of Kuramoto–Sivashinsky: tangencies and the physical modes

      Sim.lyapunov with covariant vectors: exponents with errors, covariance residual, tangencies, domination and the physical modes.

      Checked against Cvitanović et al. (2010), Ding et al. (2016).

    Workflow

    1. Record, query, reproduce: the run registry

      The run registry on a native simulation: record, certify, query, compare, reproduce bit for bit, cache only on request.

      Checked against heat equation, closed form.

    2. Importing a Dedalus script

      A convection cell as a native model, then imported from a Dedalus 3 script: line-by-line report, run, native vs translated.

      Checked against the original script (optional check, needs Dedalus).

    Run them yourself

    Once Conatus is installed with the tutorials extra (pip install "conatus[tutorials]": matplotlib for the figures, xarray for the tracers tutorial), each tutorial is a script you run with Python, on your laptop or on a cluster. The GPU part of the Rayleigh–Bénard tutorial needs a CUDA device. The same list, with when each script last ran, is the tutorials index of the documentation.
