Covariant Lyapunov vectors of Kuramoto–Sivashinsky: tangencies and the physical modes¶

The Lyapunov exponents say how fast perturbations grow. The covariant Lyapunov vectors say in which directions, and the angles between them say whether the tangent space splits cleanly. For Kuramoto–Sivashinsky on \(L = 22\), the finite-time analysis of this page finds eight entangled modes, separated from the rest. Ding et al. (2016) call these the “physical” modes. The count is an empirical result for these trajectory lengths and thresholds; check how it changes with them.

Goal. Compute the twelve leading exponents and covariant vectors of KS \(L = 22\) from a native model with sim.lyapunov, check their covariance, and count the entangled modes from the domination of the Oseledets splitting. Expected. Cvitanović, Davidchack & Siminos (2010, §2, p. 12 of arXiv:0709.2944) give the leading exponents; the first ten are \((0.048, 0, 0, -0.003, -0.189, -0.256, -0.290, -0.310, -1.963, -1.967)\), in the zero-mean subspace; the page compares its first ten estimates with them; Ding, Chaté, Cvitanović, Siminos & Takeuchi (2016): eight physical modes. Time: about two minutes on one CPU core. Script: t15_ks_covariant.py · Tests behind it: tests/chaos/.

The model and four starts¶

import numpy as np

import conatus as cn

L = 22.0
m = cn.Model(cn.Box(x=cn.periodic(L)), name="Kuramoto-Sivashinsky L=22")
m.scalar("u")
m.equations("dt(u) + lap(u) + lap(lap(u)) = -u*dx(u)")
sim = m.simulate(N=32, scheme="RK443", dt=0.05)   # 32 modes, as in the references


def start(c):
    """A smooth zero-mean initial state from four random modes."""
    return {"u": lambda x: 0.3 * sum(c[j, 0] * np.cos(2 * np.pi * (j + 1) * x / L)
                                     + c[j, 1] * np.sin(2 * np.pi * (j + 1) * x / L)
                                     for j in range(4))}


rng = np.random.default_rng(7)
starts = [start(rng.standard_normal((4, 2))) for _ in range(4)]

The starts are sim.initial keyword dicts: each becomes one trajectory of the same batch, and the simulation’s own state is left as it was.

Exponents and covariant vectors¶

res = sim.lyapunov(12, transient=100.0, align=50.0, T=200.0, reortho_every=10,
                   inner="euclidean", n_blocks=4, seed=1, starts=starts, zero_mean="u",
                   covariant=True, clv_window=40.0, clv_backward=40.0, clv_checkpoints=8)
print(res.explain())

The durations are times of the problem: transient (trajectory only), align (the tangent vectors align with the Oseledets directions), T (averaging), clv_window and clv_backward (the covariant vectors are computed over a window that ends 40 time units before the end, the time the backward iteration needs to converge). Each duration must be a whole number of steps and of intervals reortho_every * dt; otherwise the call is refused. zero_mean="u" keeps the tangent vectors in the invariant zero-mean subspace, as in the references. clv_checkpoints=8 stores 8 checkpoints instead of every interval and recomputes them once; explain() gives the memory used, the difference between recomputed and stored factors (zero), the backward convergence (the vectors obtained from two independent random starts of the backward iteration, compared: small except inside nearly degenerate pairs such as the two zero exponents, which converge slowly) and the covariance residual: each vector advanced by the tangent of the step over one interval must be parallel to the vector there; explain() gives the measured residual and its ratio to the rounding scale.

Tangencies and domination¶

dom = res.domination(tau=5.0)
print(dom.explain())
theta8 = res.angles(8)                     # span(gamma_1..gamma_8) vs the rest, every sample
theta4 = res.angles(4)
print(f"smallest angle at the split 8 | 9: {theta8.min():.3f} rad; at 4 | 5: "
      f"{theta4.min():.4f} rad ({np.mean(theta4 < 0.1):.0%} of the samples below 0.1 rad)")

domination(tau) compares the local growth rates of the vectors over windows of length \(\tau\): when \(\Lambda_i < \Lambda_j\) for \(i < j\), the splitting is not dominated there. Modes joined by such violations form clusters; the leading cluster is the set of physical modes. The principal angles tell the same story: the split \(8 \mid 9\) keeps a finite angle, the split \(4 \mid 5\) inside the physical block has near-tangencies.

Check¶

cds = np.array([0.048, 0.0, 0.0, -0.003, -0.189, -0.256, -0.290, -0.310, -1.963, -1.967])
dev = np.abs(res.exponents[:10] - cds)
assert np.all(dev < 4 * res.stderr[:10] + 0.01), (res.exponents[:10], res.stderr[:10])
# J gamma_i(t) is parallel to gamma_i(t + dt_interval), to the rounding floor of each vector
assert np.all(res.clv_covariance_residual < 100 * res.clv_covariance_floor)
assert dom.n_physical == 8 and dom.violation(8) == 0.0
assert res.clv_split_convergence[7] < 1e-6            # the split 8 | 9 itself has converged
assert theta8.min() > 10 * theta4.min()               # isolated split versus tangencies
print("checks passed: exponents within their errors of CDS 2010, eight physical modes")
