Bifurcations: the Lorenz equilibria¶

Goal. Follow the equilibria of the Lorenz system as \(r\) grows, through the pitchfork at \(r = 1\) (with a declared symmetry) and the Hopf point, and compute the first Lyapunov coefficient there. Expected. Lorenz (1963): pitchfork at \(r = 1\) with \(x^2 = b(r-1)\) on \(C^\pm\); Hopf at \(r_H = \sigma(\sigma+b+3)/(\sigma-b-1) = 470/19\) with \(\omega^2 = b(\sigma + r_H)\), subcritical (Marsden & McCracken 1976). Extensions. Three later sections build on this result; each can be read alone. The cycles born at the Hopf point. The Bogdanov–Takens normal form in two parameters (Kuznetsov 2004, §8.4): fold curve \(\beta_1 = \beta_2^2/4\), Hopf curve \(\beta_1 = 0\), both ending at \((0,0)\). The Landau and Ginzburg–Landau coefficients of Swift–Hohenberg rolls (Cross & Hohenberg 1993): \(A^2 = 4r/3\), \(\tau_0 = 1\), \(\xi_0^2 = 4\), \(g_0 = 3\). Time: under a minute on one CPU core. Script: t10_bifurcations.py · Tests behind it: tests/bifurcation/test_bifurcation_core.py, tests/bifurcation/test_amplitude.py.

The core result ends with “Normal forms”: the Hopf point and the sign of its Lyapunov coefficient.

Write the system¶

import numpy as np

import conatus as cn
from conatus.expert import (EVEN, IVP, CollocationMesh, ContinuationSettings, Reflection,
                            SymmetrySubspace, amplitude_equation, branch_switch, continue_codim2,
                            continue_equilibria, cos_mode_observable, ginzburg_landau, ir_system,
                            normal_form, ode_problem)

lorenz = ode_problem(["x", "y", "z"], {"sigma": 10.0, "r": 0.5, "b": 8 / 3},
                     lambda X, P: [P["sigma"] * (X["y"] - X["x"]),
                                   P["r"] * X["x"] - X["y"] - X["x"] * X["z"],
                                   X["x"] * X["y"] - P["b"] * X["z"]], name="Lorenz")
L = ir_system(lorenz, ["r"])          # G(x; r), exact J, dG/dr, and the multilinear forms B, C
print(L.explain())

  • ode_problem writes an ODE from its right-hand side, given as a Python function of the state and the parameters. A PDE written as a native model works the same way (Swift–Hohenberg below).

  • ir_system discretizes once and exposes \(M\dot x = G(x;p)\). The Jacobian and the multilinear forms \(B = F''\), \(C = F'''\) are symbolic Fréchet derivatives of the equations, with no finite differences in \(x\). \(\partial G/\partial r\) is symbolic too: \(r\) enters linearly, and explain() says so.

Equilibria and branch switching with a declared symmetry¶

cfg = ContinuationSettings(ds=0.1, ds_min=1e-9, ds_max=2.0, adapt=1.3, fast_iterations=4,
                           tolerance=1e-11, max_newton=12, max_points=60, dp_fd=None)
origin = continue_equilibria(L, np.zeros(3), "r", cfg, marginal_tol=1e-9, bounds=(0, 3))
(bp,) = origin.events_of("branch point")
S = np.diag([-1.0, -1.0, 1.0])        # (x, y, z) -> (-x, -y, z): a symmetry of Lorenz, declared
cplus = branch_switch(origin, bp, cfg, step=0.05, symmetry=S, bounds=(0, 30))
print(cplus.explain())
(hopf,) = cplus.events_of("hopf")

continue_equilibria evaluates three test functions at each point (fold: the parameter component of the tangent; branch point: the determinant of the bordered Jacobian; Hopf: a product over eigenvalue pairs) and locates each sign change by a secant, reporting the residual there. branch_switch solves the algebraic branching equation with the second derivatives. The declared \(S = \mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,1)\) classifies the point as a symmetry-breaking pitchfork: the kernel is \(S\)-odd, and the two half-branches \(C^\pm\) are images of each other. The symmetry declaration and the branching equation determine the two branch directions.

Normal forms¶

pf = normal_form(L, bp.point.z[:-1], kind="zero", par={"r": bp.point.z[-1]}, parameter="r", symmetry=S)
hf = normal_form(L, hopf.point.z[:-1], kind="hopf", par={"r": hopf.point.z[-1]}, parameter="r")
print(pf.explain())
print(hf.explain())

The centre-manifold projection (Kuznetsov) gives the pitchfork cubic coefficient and, at the Hopf point, \(c_1\) and the first Lyapunov coefficient \(\ell_1 = \mathrm{Re}\,c_1/\omega\): positive here, so the Hopf bifurcation of Lorenz is subcritical. Each quantity is reported with its checks (residuals of the eigenvectors and of the bordered solves); the normalization \(\|q\| = 1\) is declared.

Extension: cycles from the Hopf point¶

cfo = ContinuationSettings(ds=0.2, ds_min=1e-9, ds_max=1.0, adapt=1.3, fast_iterations=4,
                           tolerance=1e-9, max_newton=12, max_points=12, dp_fd=None)
cycles = branch_switch(cplus, hopf, cfo, amplitude=0.2, mesh=CollocationMesh.uniform(40, 4),
                       tests=("lpc",))
print(cycles.explain())

The first cycle is predicted by the normal form (amplitude declared, \(\Delta r = -\mathrm{Re}\,c_1|z|^2/\alpha'\)), corrected by orthogonal collocation and continued (Doedel, Keller & Kernévez 1991; Continuation and bifurcations). Subcritical: the cycles exist for \(r < r_H\). Followed further, their period diverges at the homoclinic explosion \(r \approx 13.926\).

Extension: fold and Hopf curves in two parameters¶

bt = ir_system(ode_problem(["x", "y"], {"b1": -0.5, "b2": -1.0},
                           lambda X, P: [X["y"], P["b1"] + P["b2"] * X["x"] + X["x"] * X["x"]
                                         - X["x"] * X["y"]], name="Bogdanov-Takens"), ["b1", "b2"])
cfg2 = ContinuationSettings(ds=0.05, ds_min=1e-9, ds_max=0.3, adapt=1.3, fast_iterations=3,
                            tolerance=1e-11, max_newton=12, max_points=40, dp_fd=None)
eq = continue_equilibria(bt, np.array([(1 - np.sqrt(3)) / 2, 0.0]), "b1", cfg2, marginal_tol=1e-9,
                         bounds=(-2, 2))
fold_pt, hopf_pt = eq.events_of("fold")[0], eq.events_of("hopf")[0]
fold_curve = continue_codim2(bt, fold_pt.point.z[:-1], "fold", ("b1", "b2"), cfg2,
                             par={"b1": fold_pt.point.z[-1]}, bounds={"b2": (-1.5, 1.5)})
hopf_curve = continue_codim2(bt, hopf_pt.point.z[:-1], "hopf", ("b1", "b2"), cfg2,
                             par={"b1": hopf_pt.point.z[-1]}, bounds={"b2": (-1.5, 1.5)})
print(fold_curve.explain())
print(hopf_curve.explain())

Minimally augmented systems (Govaerts): one scalar condition \(g = 0\) from a bordered matrix (fold), two for Hopf (bordered \(J^2 + \kappa I\), \(\kappa = \omega^2\)). Along the curves, test functions detect cusp, Bogdanov–Takens, Bautin, fold–Hopf and Hopf–Hopf points. The fold curve passes through the BT point. The Hopf curve ends there: \(\kappa = \omega^2\) reaches 0, and for \(\beta_2 > 0\) the equilibrium has a real pair of eigenvalues \(\pm\sqrt{\beta_2}\), which the augmented equations also satisfy with \(\kappa < 0\). The continuation stops at \(\kappa = 0\), and every point it keeps is checked against the eigenvalues of the Jacobian, computed on their own. The vanishing determinant at the BT point is not reported as a fold–Hopf point (rejected with its reason).

Extension: Landau and Ginzburg–Landau equations of a PDE¶

def swift_hohenberg(k, N=16):
    """Swift-Hohenberg on one wavelength 2 pi / k, as a native model; r is the free parameter."""
    m = cn.Model(cn.Box(x=cn.periodic(2 * np.pi / k)), name="Swift-Hohenberg", r=0.0)
    m.scalar("u")                                          # the unknown
    m.define("S(f) = f + dx(dx(f))")                       # (1 + d_xx), a named operator
    m.equations("dt(u) - r*u + S(S(u)) = -u*u*u")
    c = m.compile(N, dealias=2)                            # u^3: dealias 2, declared
    # The even subspace u(-x) = u(x) (pins the phase of the rolls) is declared on the compiled
    # problem: the native layer has no symmetry= option yet.
    even = IVP(c.problem.variables, name=c.problem.name,
               symmetry=SymmetrySubspace(Reflection(c.coordsys["x"], about=0), u=EVEN))
    for eq in c.problem.equations:
        even.add_equation(eq)
    s = ir_system(even, ["r"])
    return s, np.zeros(s.n)


sh, u0 = swift_hohenberg(1.0)
cos_coeff = cos_mode_observable(sh, "u")          # A = coefficient of cos x in u (linear functional)
landau = amplitude_equation(sh, u0, "r", kind="stationary", observable=cos_coeff)
gl = ginzburg_landau(swift_hohenberg, 1.0, "r", kind="stationary", observable=cos_coeff, h=1e-2)
print(landau.explain())
print(gl.explain())

Swift–Hohenberg is a native model: the operator \(1 + \partial_{xx}\) is named once with m.define, the equation is written as on paper, and dealias=2 is written out although it is the native default for a cubic nonlinearity ((p+1)/2 with p = 3): the published coefficients do not depend on a default. The even subspace \(u(-x) = u(x)\) removes the translation mode; it is declared on the compiled problem (expert layer), because the native layer has no symmetry= option yet. amplitude_equation rewrites the pitchfork normal form for an observable amplitude (here the \(\cos x\) coefficient), and ginzburg_landau adds the curvature of the growth rate \(\sigma(k)\) (systems rebuilt at \(k_c \pm h\), \(\pm 2h\), fourth-order differences, step declared). The same call on stress-free Rayleigh–Bénard rolls gives \(\mathrm{Nu} - 1 = 2\varepsilon\) (Malkus & Veronis 1958) with spectral convergence in \(N_z\): examples/bifurcation/weakly_nonlinear.py.
